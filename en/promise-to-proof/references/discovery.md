# Discovery

Load before any first call, and for customer research before building an offer. Closes with `templates/discovery-call-guide.md`. Worked contrast: `examples/discovery-call-transcript.md`.

## Purpose

Discovery builds the gap (`references/qualification.md`): current state, future state, cost of the gap, root cause. It is not a pitch with questions attached. The seller talks ~40% or less; the buyer talks the rest. Gong's published call analyses associate higher win rates with 11–14 questions per discovery call, mixed across types; treat this as a prompt to prepare more questions than you think you need, not as a quota.

## SPIN sequence

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Facts you couldn't find yourself. Keep to 2–3; buyers resent being interviewed about what's on their website | "How is the team split between X and Y today?" |
| **Problem** | Surface difficulties and dissatisfactions | "Where does the current process break down?" |
| **Implication** | Make the problem's consequences explicit and bigger. This is where value is created | "When that happens, what does it cost in hours? Who gets pulled in? What does it do to the quarter?" |
| **Need-payoff** | Let the buyer state the value of a solution in their own words | "If that were fixed, what would it let you do? How would you know it worked?" |

Most sellers skip Implication and jump to Need-payoff, which produces polite agreement and no urgency. Spend most of the call in Problem and Implication.

## The Mom Test (for customer research and early discovery)

Three rules that stop people from lying to you politely:
1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Questions about their past behavior, workflow and decisions; the idea stays in your pocket.
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future.** "When did this last happen? What did you do?" beats "Would you use X?"
3. **Talk less, listen more.** Compliments and hypotheticals are noise; commitments (time, reputation, money) are signal.

Research questions that pass the test:
- "Tell me about the last time [problem] happened. What did you do next?"
- "What have you tried? What happened? Why did you stop?"
- "Who else was involved in that decision? How long did it take?"
- "What are you spending on this today, in money and in hours?"
- "What would have to be true for you to change how you do this?"

End research conversations with a commitment ask (an intro, a follow-up meeting with a decision maker, a pilot), not a thank-you. If they won't commit anything, the pain is mild.

## Question bank (30)

**Open the call**
1. "What prompted you to take this call?"
2. "What would make this worth 30 minutes for you?"

**Situation (max 3)**
3. "How does [process] run today, start to finish?"
4. "Who owns it, and who else touches it?"
5. "What tools or vendors are involved?"

**Problem**
6. "Where does it break down most often?"
7. "What do people complain about?"
8. "What have you already tried?"
9. "What stopped that from working?"
10. "What's the workaround people use?"

**Implication**
11. "When it breaks, what does it cost in time? Who absorbs it?"
12. "How does that show up in the numbers you report?"
13. "Has it ever caused a missed deadline, a lost customer, a compliance issue?"
14. "If nothing changes, what does next year look like?"
15. "How does this affect you personally — your goals, your team's morale?"
16. "Who above you notices this? What do they say?"

**Need-payoff**
17. "If this were solved, what would your team do with the time?"
18. "How would you measure that it worked?"
19. "What would that be worth over a year, roughly?"
20. "Who else would benefit?"

**Decision and process**
21. "How did you buy something like this last time? What went well, what didn't?"
22. "Who needs to be involved for a decision like this?"
23. "What criteria will you use to compare options?"
24. "What happens between 'yes' and a signed contract here?"
25. "Is there a date this needs to be solved by? What's driving it?"

**Alternatives**
26. "What else are you considering, including doing nothing?"
27. "What would make you build this in-house instead?"

**Close the call**
28. "What did I miss?"
29. "Based on what you've shared, here's what I'd suggest as a next step: [specific]. Does that work?"
30. "Who should join that next conversation?"

## Continuous discovery

Discovery doesn't end after call one. Each stage re-opens it: new stakeholders bring new pains, the paper process reveals new criteria, a competitor's demo changes the comparison. Keep the MEDDPICC scorecard live and update it after every touch.

## What to capture (notes template)

- Pain in their words (quote).
- Numbers: current, target, cost of gap.
- Named people and roles mentioned.
- Decision criteria and process as stated.
- Alternatives named.
- Commitments made by them and by you.
- Next step with date.

## Anti-patterns

- Opening with a company intro or "hope you're well".
- Asking what you could have read on their website.
- Pitching at the first sign of a problem.
- Accepting "we want to improve efficiency" as a pain. Ask what, how much, since when.
- Ending without a scheduled next step.
