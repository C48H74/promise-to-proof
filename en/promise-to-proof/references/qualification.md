# Qualification

Load for enterprise or large deals, pipeline reviews, and whenever a deal "feels good" but has no evidence. Closes with `templates/meddpicc-scorecard.md`.

## Why BANT is demoted

Budget, Authority, Need, Timing were designed for transactional selling. In complex deals budgets get created when pain is quantified, authority is distributed across a committee, and timing is a function of the paper process. Keep budget and timing as *signals* in the notes; never gate a deal on them.

## MEDDPICC — operational definitions

Each letter needs **evidence** (something the buyer said or sent), not inference. Score: green = evidence in writing or from two people; yellow = said once by one person; red = assumed or unknown.

| Letter | What it means operationally | Evidence looks like | Question that gets it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** Metrics | The economic size of the problem and of the fix | A number with a unit and a period ("we lose ~40 hours/week", "renewals dropped 12%") | "How do you measure this today? What's the number now, and what would good look like?" |
| **E** Economic buyer | The person who can release money without asking anyone | Their name, and your direct contact with them | "Who ultimately signs off on spend like this? When could we involve them?" |
| **D** Decision criteria | The written list they will score vendors against | A document, an RFP, or a list they dictated | "What will you compare options on? Who wrote those criteria?" |
| **D** Decision process | Steps, people and dates from "yes" to signature, including legal, security, procurement | A sequence with dates | "Walk me through what happens after you decide. Who else has to approve? How long did the last purchase like this take?" |
| **P** Pain identified | The problem in the buyer's words, quantified | A quote plus a number | "What happens if this isn't fixed this year?" |
| **I** Implicate the pain | The buyer links the pain to consequences for them personally and the business | They say it, you don't | "Who feels this most? What does it mean for your goals?" |
| **C** Champion | Someone with influence who sells for you when you're not there, and has something to gain | They set up meetings you asked for, share internal docs, warn you about risks | Test: ask them to do something internal for you. A champion does it; a friendly contact explains why they can't |
| **C** Competition | Every alternative, including status quo and building in-house | Named options and their perceived strengths | "What else are you considering? What would you do if you did nothing?" |

**Paper process** (legal, security questionnaire, procurement portal, vendor onboarding) is part of Decision process and routinely adds 2–8 weeks. Map it before the proposal, not after.

## Gap selling

The sale lives in the gap between current state and future state:

1. **Current state:** facts about how things work today, with numbers, and the business impact of those facts.
2. **Future state:** what they want to be true, with numbers.
3. **Gap:** the difference, in money, time or risk.
4. **Root cause:** why the gap exists (you can only sell what fixes a root cause).

Without a quantified gap, there is no urgency and no price justification. Discovery (`references/discovery.md`) exists to build this picture.

## Disqualify early

Disqualify when any of these are true after a real attempt to fix them:
- No pain the buyer can state in their own words.
- No access to anyone who can release money.
- The decision process is "we'll see" after two asks.
- Your solution doesn't fix the root cause.
- Capacity: you couldn't deliver well even if they signed.

A fast, polite no protects pipeline hygiene and the relationship. Script: "From what you've described, this isn't the right fit right now because [reason]. If [trigger] changes, I'd like to hear from you."

## Stage gates

See `SKILL.md`. The gates are what prevent a forecast built on hope. A deal does not advance because the seller is optimistic; it advances when evidence exists for the next gate.

## Deal size rule

- Below ~1 month of the buyer's discretionary budget: use Pain, Metrics, Decision process only; the full scorecard is overkill.
- Above that, or any committee purchase: full MEDDPICC plus `templates/buying-committee-map.md`.

## Pipeline exit evidence by stage

Use [deal review](../checklists/deal-review.md) and [mutual action plan](../templates/mutual-action-plan.md). Adapt the stage model to transaction size and actual buying process; a self-serve purchase need not pass through a live proposal.

| Stage | Evidence to advance | Hold/stop signal |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant lead | Fit hypothesis, legitimate contact route and reason to engage | Refusal, no relevant trigger or wrong contact |
| Discovery/qualification | Buyer recognizes a problem; offer can help; next evaluation path | No fit, no meaningful problem or unsupported required outcome |
| Diagnosis/evaluation | Agreed criteria, baseline/assumptions, owners and feasible validation | Missing mandatory capability or unresolved rollout dependency |
| Proposal/commercial review | Scope, price, terms, limits and buying path understood | Comparison/affordability/committee uncertainty requires resolution |
| Authorized decision/agreement | Actual required approvals and agreement; dependencies/start verified | A verbal hint or "send contract" is not closed-won |
| Delivery/activation | Accepted first useful result with evidence | Onboarding or quality failure |
| Retention/expansion | Durable value, viable contribution and actual additional need | Churn, oversold promise or unfunded support |

CRM fields: source/date, buyer problem, fit evidence, criteria/committee, next action/owner/date, stage-entry date, offer/terms version, evidence status and reason for hold/loss. Track no-decision separately from competitor and no-fit, including unknown outcomes.

Measure conversion on eligible stage entrants and complete observation windows; show counts. Wins divided by concluded opportunities can differ from proposals eventually won. Define the denominator rather than comparing incompatible rates.

Recommend a live proposal discussion where it resolves ambiguity; support written review and procurement. For transparent standard pricing, do not withhold prices until an elaborate case is complete. Quantified value claims need buyer-supported inputs.

At each stage ask: what decision has the buyer actually made, what remains uncertain and which action resolves it? Seller activity alone (deck sent, demo held) is not buying progress. Avoid endless follow-up when there is no viable permission-based next action.

## Handoff to delivery

A deal is not closed until the handoff contract is signed by both owners (`references/handoff-sell-to-deliver.md`, `templates/handoff-contract.md`). Closed-won with no delivery owner is an open risk, not a win.
