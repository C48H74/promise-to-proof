# Website and content

Load for "what should I publish", website reviews, and content that supports deals.

## Order of work

1. **Key pages first:** services/pricing/contact pages that answer the buyer's questions, name objections and show proof. Until these convert, content sends traffic to a leaky page.
2. **Bottom of funnel:** pieces that answer the question a buyer has at the moment of decision (comparisons, "how we work", pricing logic, implementation timeline, security/compliance answers, "who this is not for"). Use after discovery calls, in follow-ups, after proposals.
3. **Top of funnel:** reach and recall, broader topics than the service. Only after 1 and 2 exist.

## Rules

- Teach only what you have done. Case-backed content outperforms opinion content with buyers who have money.
- Show the reasoning, not just conclusions; buyers hire the judgment, not the information.
- Write for one reader; cut hedging; every page must survive the back button.
- The "about" page is the only page about you. Every other page is about the reader's outcome and objections.
- Sustainable cadence (one solid piece every two weeks beats daily noise). Client conversations are the editorial calendar.
- Update old pieces; compounding comes from maintenance.
- Deliver what the title promises.
- Measure trust-building interactions (replies, referrals, inbound meetings), not volume metrics alone.
- Include expert quotes and co-authored pieces: better quality and shared distribution.

## Content that moves deals

| Deal stage | Piece |
|---|---|
| After discovery | "How we diagnose [problem]" with a worked example |
| Proposal | Business case one-pager for the economic buyer |
| Committee | Two-slide summary the champion forwards |
| Security/legal | Pre-written answers to the standard questionnaire |
| Negotiation | Implementation timeline and what the client must provide |
| Post-sale | 30/60/90 recap template; value recap emails |

## AI and discoverability

Check how AI assistants describe your company and category; correct gaps with clear, factual pages (what you do, for whom, pricing logic, proof). Details: `references/ai-in-sales.md`.

## Anti-patterns

- Publishing before key pages convert.
- Thought-leadership with no evidence.
- Content calendars built on keywords instead of buyer questions.
- Gated PDFs nobody downloads twice.
