# Persuasion mechanics, used honestly

Load for phrasing, for understanding why a buyer behaves as they do, and to check that a tactic isn't manipulation.

## Cialdini's principles and their honest application

| Principle | Honest use | Dishonest use (don't) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | Give real value first: a diagnosis, a template, an intro | Gifts to create obligation |
| **Commitment and consistency** | Small, real commitments (a next step, a pilot) that lead to larger ones; restate what they said they wanted | Trick questions to extract a "yes" |
| **Social proof** | Named references in the same segment, documented results | Vague "hundreds of companies", invented logos |
| **Authority** | Evidence of expertise: published thinking, credentials, results | Titles without substance |
| **Liking** | Genuine interest, similarity found honestly | Flattery |
| **Scarcity** | Real capacity limits, real deadlines (`references/guarantees-scarcity.md`) | Fake countdowns |
| **Unity** | Shared identity when true ("as fellow founders…") | Manufactured belonging |

Test: would the buyer feel respected if they saw your notes on the tactic? If not, don't.

## Decision biases to recognize (not exploit)

- **Status quo bias:** the current way feels safer. Counter with quantified cost of inaction, not with pressure.
- **Loss aversion:** losses weigh more than equivalent gains. Frame outcomes truthfully in both directions.
- **Choice overload:** more options reduce decisions. Narrow to 2–3 (`references/jolt.md`).
- **Anchoring:** the first number sets the frame. Anchor with the full price and the outcome.

## Exact phrases (Phil M. Jones style) that open conversation without pressure

- "Just so I understand…" (before restating their situation)
- "How would it sound if…" (introducing an option)
- "What do you know about…" (testing knowledge without lecturing)
- "Most people in your position…" (social proof + permission to be normal)
- "Before you decide…" (holds attention for one more fact)
- "Would it be a bad idea to…" (a no-oriented question; easy to answer)
- "What would need to be true for…" (surfaces real criteria)

## Emotional intelligence in the call (Blount, *Sales EQ*)

- Subordinate your need to be right or to win the argument; the buyer's need to feel heard comes first.
- Watch for the buyer's state: anxious buyers need fewer options and more certainty; bored buyers need an insight; defensive buyers need an accusation audit (`references/objections.md`).
- Your emotional discipline is visible: calm after an objection, no rush after a yes.

## Radical transparency

State who the offer is *not* for, what it won't do, and where it failed before. Buyers discount claims; they trust admitted limits. Put a "when this isn't the right fit" section in proposals and on the website (`references/delivery-retention.md`, `references/content.md`).

## Anti-patterns

- Memorized "closing lines".
- Using urgency on fear-of-failure buyers.
- Techniques the buyer would resent if they recognized them.
