# Operating economics, capacity and cash

Use [dashboard](../templates/metrics-dashboard.md) and [worked calculations](../examples/unit-economics.md). State currency, period and cost treatment. These are management calculations, not tax/accounting conclusions.

- Revenue per unit = actual net selling price, excluding pass-through taxes when appropriate.
- Contribution per unit = revenue − variable delivery/support/refund/payment costs. Report acquisition separately or explicitly label contribution **after acquisition**; never subtract CAC twice.
- Contribution margin = contribution / revenue. Markup = (price − chosen cost base) / chosen cost base; define that base.
- Break-even units = period fixed costs / positive unit contribution; round up. If contribution is nonpositive, no finite volume covers fixed costs under this model.
- Capacity = available bottleneck hours / hours required per unit, adjusted for known rework and other commitments. Confirm labor, equipment and start cadence separately.

Normalize owner/founder labor at a stated realistic rate; ignoring unpaid labor exaggerates scalability. A contribution estimate excludes fixed overhead unless explicitly allocated; do not call it net profit.

Cash schedule: deposits, acquisition outflow, delivery payroll/suppliers, milestones, refunds and actual payment dates. A positive project margin can still exceed working-capital limits. Annual prepayment changes cash timing, not necessarily profitability or lifetime value.

Guarantee expected cost = covered failures × expected remedy cost, with evidence for probability. Also calculate worst-case exposure and reserve/approval needs. Avoid pretending a modeled failure rate is observed.

Analyze downside/base/upside cases for scope, rework, conversion, payment delay and retention. Acquisition spend must fit contribution and financing constraints. Segment by customer/channel where practical; blended economics can conceal a loss-making segment.

Example: raising prices while doubling rework may reduce contribution and capacity. Investigate actual fulfillment before treating price as the only lever. Choose changes based on economics **and** customer fit, not a universal 5–10× cost rule.
