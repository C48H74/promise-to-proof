# Proof, case studies and limits

Use [case-study template](../templates/case-study.md) and [risk/proof review](../checklists/risk-and-proof.md). V1's Klettke influence is retained as provenance in the [reading map](reading-by-section.md); examples/checklists here are original.

Evidence chain: baseline → intervention → measured result → period/sample → attribution → limitations → publication approval. Keep raw evidence or a lawful accessible pointer; distinguish customer-reported benefit from independently verified measurement.

A good case explains initial situation, why this action was chosen, implementation constraints, result and what did not work. Do not select only premium customers automatically; relevance and evidence matter more than spend. Include who the product does not serve and dependencies needed to replicate the process.

For financial benefits distinguish potential, attributable and realized/captured benefit. Time released is not necessarily cash saved; reduced risk is not a counted incident prevented without a defensible counterfactual. Avoid double-counting the same benefit as both labor savings and revenue.

Ask specifically which useful result could be featured, explain interview effort and review/approval process, and confirm names, quotes, metrics, images and channels separately. Store approval scope/date. Do not promise universal customer editorial control beyond the actual agreement or publish before relevant authorization.

If no cases exist, use labeled simulations, product demonstrations or attributed public examples. They demonstrate a method, not customer traction. Certifications support only their verified scope. A before/after result without controls may be persuasive evidence, but it does not establish causal certainty.

Publish proof near the decision it helps resolve, with visible caveats. Recheck when product capabilities, customer permissions or facts change.
