# Diagnose before prescribing

Fill the [engine diagnostic](../templates/engine-diagnostic.md).

Map the actual flow: relevant reach → response → qualified opportunity → proposal/trial → decision → activation/delivery → retained customer → expansion/referral. Keep counts, conversion windows and median time per stage. Separate competitor loss, no-fit, no-budget, no-decision and unknown reasons; a CRM guess is not buyer-confirmed evidence.

| Observation | Competing explanation | Evidence | Candidate test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Few relevant conversations | Distribution, trigger or reachable segment | Coverage, target list, replies | One segment/message/channel pilot |
| Many replies, few qualified | Wrong audience or misleading promise | Fit/problem evidence | Narrow trigger and exclusions |
| Qualified deals stall | Value, committee, risk or procurement | Deferred interviews, stakeholder map | Resolve one missing criterion |
| Deals lose money | Price, scope, rework or capacity | Actual delivery cost | Scope/price/capacity redesign |
| Activation but weak renewal | Transient value or false success event | Cohort use, exit interviews | First durable result |

Investigate the largest economically meaningful leak, not merely the lowest percentage. A valuable segment may justify a slower cycle; a capacity-limited service may need fewer leads. Report counts and uncertainty for small samples.

Write: "Leading explanation: __; evidence: __; alternative: __; missing information: __; smallest distinguishing test: __." Pick one primary change, outcome metric, guardrails and review date using the [experiment card](../templates/experiment-card.md).

Example: 40 inquiries, 6 fit, 4 proposals, 2 sales and overloaded delivery suggests lead-fit and capacity issues. It does not prove a weak offer or sales script. Separate marketing opportunity from delivery feasibility.
