# Cold email: bad vs. good

Method: `references/outbound.md`, template: `templates/5-touch-sequence.md`.

## Bad

> Subject: Revolutionary AI platform to 10x your sales!
>
> Hi there,
>
> I hope this email finds you well. My name is Alex and I'm the founder of AcmeSoft, a leading provider of cutting-edge AI-powered sales solutions. We help companies like yours increase revenue, reduce costs and improve efficiency. Our platform has 50+ features including...
>
> Do you have 30 minutes this week for a demo?

**Why it fails**
- Subject promises a result nobody believes; spam-filter bait.
- "Hi there": no research, no name.
- Opens with the sender, not the reader.
- Claims ("increase revenue, reduce costs") apply to everyone, so they land with no one.
- Feature list before any problem is named.
- Ask is 30 minutes of the buyer's time for a demo of something they didn't ask about.

## Good

> Subject: onboarding time at Brightline
>
> Maria, I saw Brightline opened two support hubs this quarter and is hiring 12 agents.
>
> Teams that scale support that fast usually see new-agent ramp time become the bottleneck: first-month quality drops and senior agents get pulled into coaching.
>
> We helped a similar-sized team cut ramp from 9 weeks to 5 (details on request).
>
> Is ramp time something you're watching, or is it handled?

**Why it works**
- Subject is lowercase, specific, internal-looking, and about them.
- A verifiable signal (hubs, hiring) shows research, not a mail merge.
- Names a likely problem in their language; one claim, one proof point.
- Ask is a yes/no question, low effort, easy to answer honestly ("not a priority" is a useful reply).
- Under 80 words, plain text, no links or attachments on touch 1.

## Checklist before you send
- [ ] Would this email make sense only for this person? If not, rewrite the first line.
- [ ] One problem, one proof point, one question.
- [ ] No attachment, no tracking-heavy formatting, ≤ 100 words.
- [ ] Sender domain has SPF/DKIM/DMARC; volume within caps in `references/outbound.md`.
- [ ] Number in the proof point is real and you can show the source.

## Follow-up (touch 2, 3 days later)

> Maria, one more angle: the teams that fixed ramp fastest usually changed what new agents practice in week one, not how long training lasts. Happy to share the one-page version if useful.

Adds a new idea; does not say "just bumping this".
